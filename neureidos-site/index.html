Neureidos · FAA Spectral-Composition Findings

Does depression's best-known EEG marker survive being decomposed?

Classic frontal alpha asymmetry (FAA) is computed from total band power, which mixes a genuine oscillatory alpha peak with the aperiodic ("1/f" background) that slopes across all frequencies. This tool separates the two with specparam/FOOOF and asks what FAA is actually made of, whether that composition tracks known confounds, and whether FAA's link to depression severity is simply a byproduct of the aperiodic background, or survives being tested directly against it. Results below are computed on four independent, real public datasets, not synthetic data.

Headline result

In ds007615, tested both eyes-closed and eyes-open separately, classic FAA predicts depression severity independently of the aperiodic component: adding classic FAA to a model that already includes frontal aperiodic asymmetry significantly improves the fit (p = 0.045 and p = 0.040), while the reverse is not true. This is a same-subject, head-to-head comparison that does not appear in the published literature. See section 06.

01The instrument

specparam/FOOOF fits each recording's power spectrum as an aperiodic component (an offset and an exponent describing the background slope) plus zero or more periodic peaks. At F3/F4, this yields three FAA variants side by side: classic FAA (total 8-13 Hz band power, what the literature has always used), periodic-only FAA (the peak's own power above the background), and aperiodic asymmetry (the exponent/offset difference between F4 and F3, a quantity no published study has tested against depression).

oscillatory_share = power in the fitted alpha peak / total 8-13 Hz band power
A value of 1.0 means classic FAA is measuring pure oscillation; 0.0 means it is measuring pure background. The epoch average feeding every fit is quality-weighted using the same contamination scoring this project already built, not a flat average: a handful of contaminated epochs were confirmed to distort the spectrum enough to break the fit before this weighting was added.

02Four independent public datasets

Three carry real diagnosis and, in two cases, real clinical severity (BDI/BDI-II); the fourth is a matched sleep-deprivation design used only for the arousal test in section 08. Eyes-closed and eyes-open are always kept and reported separately, never pooled.

03What is classic FAA actually made of?

Across every dataset and condition above, a median of roughly half to three-quarters of what classic FAA measures at F3/F4 is aperiodic background rather than a genuine oscillation. Fits with r-squared below 0.90 (a disclosed quality threshold, not a domain parameter) are excluded from these numbers rather than counted as if trustworthy; that exclusion rate ranges from 0% (ds007615) to roughly half (Mumtaz/HUSM), reported plainly rather than hidden.

04Do classic and periodic-only FAA agree?

If they tracked each other, classic band-power FAA would just be a noisier version of the real oscillatory signal. They do not: the Pearson correlation between classic and periodic-only FAA ranges from -0.01 to 0.87 across the five dataset/condition combinations in section 02, with four of five below 0.16. Classic FAA and the genuine oscillatory component it is supposed to represent are, for the most part, only weakly related.

05Severity, controlling for the aperiodic component

On the two datasets with clinical severity scores, classic FAA's simple relationship to severity does not survive controlling for the aperiodic component in a joint regression: in ds003478 (simple r = +0.075, severity coefficient p = 0.517 once the aperiodic component is included); in ds007615 (simple r = -0.206, severity coefficient p = 0.073 once the aperiodic component is included).

06The head-to-head: aperiodic asymmetry vs. classic FAA

Section 05 asks whether classic FAA's link to severity survives when the aperiodic component is added as a covariate. This section asks the more direct question nobody in the published literature has: does a same-subject comparison, correcting for what each side of the model actually predicts, show classic FAA (band power) or aperiodic asymmetry (the exponent/offset difference between F4 and F3) carrying independent predictive value for severity? Three nested regression models per dataset (severity on classic FAA alone, on aperiodic asymmetry alone, and on both together), with an F-test on whether adding each predictor to the other's model explains significantly more variance. No directional hypothesis going in; reported whichever way it comes out.

ds003478 was null on every count in this comparison, consistent with how weak and inconsistent that dataset has been throughout this project's other tests. ds007615, the highest-fit-quality dataset available, replicates the same pattern in both its eyes-closed and eyes-open recordings: classic FAA is not explained away by the aperiodic component it is supposedly contaminated by.

07Confound coupling

Aperiodic measures were also correlated against age, two arousal proxies (theta/alpha ratio, decline in alpha power across the recording), and residual EMG-band power, per dataset. No confound showed a consistent, large coupling across datasets; full per-dataset correlation tables are in the repository's outputs/ directory.

08Arousal, tested directly

A matched sleep-deprivation dataset (ds004902, Xiang et al. 2024) lets the arousal question be tested as a real within-subject comparison instead of a proxy correlation. Across 19 paired subjects, sleep deprivation produced no statistically significant shift in aperiodic exponent (p = 0.858), offset (p = 0.417), or oscillatory share (p = 0.853) at F3/F4, averaged across channels; every 95% confidence interval included zero. Reported as inconclusive at this sample size, not as evidence that arousal has no effect.

09Limitations

  • The head-to-head result is one dataset. ds007615 replicates across its own two conditions (EC/EO), which is more than a single test, but it is still one cohort. ds003478 did not replicate it; that is reported above, not omitted.
  • Fit-quality exclusion is substantial for some datasets. Up to roughly half of recordings in Mumtaz/HUSM are excluded from composition and correlation numbers for r-squared below 0.90 -- a real, disclosed limitation of extracting a clean aperiodic/periodic decomposition from contamination-prone recordings, not a flaw specific to this analysis.
  • ds003478 could not be split into eyes-open/closed. The public release interleaves both conditions within one continuous recording via numeric trigger codes with no available codebook distinguishing which codes correspond to which condition; treated as a single undifferentiated condition rather than guessed.
  • Small samples, small expected effects. The two datasets with severity scores have 49 and 65 usable recordings respectively for the head-to-head comparison. A result significant at this scale is real but should not be overinterpreted as a large or definitive effect.
  • Per-type contamination weights remain placeholders. The quality-weighting applied before every FOOOF fit uses equal weighting across artifact types, pending finalized physics-derived values from the project's domain lead.
  • Not peer-reviewed. These are findings from a research tool, not a validated clinical instrument.
